# Presenting Calm Navigation Companion for testing

This is Stage 15 (informal validation) — the point of this session is to find out if the prototype is wrong, not to convince someone it's right. Watch behavior more than you listen to opinions; what people actually do with it is more honest than what they say about it.

---

## Opening (30 seconds, say this or close to it)

> "This is a prototype for a project on sensory-friendly design in public spaces — it's not a finished product, and I'm not attached to it. I want to watch you actually use it and hear what's confusing or annoying, not just whether you like the idea. There's no wrong answer, and if something feels broken or pointless, that's exactly what I need to hear."

Why this framing: it lowers the social pressure to be polite about your own work, and it sets up permission to say "this doesn't make sense" without it feeling like an attack.

## Before you hand over the phone

- Ask: **"Do you tend to need quiet to focus, or do you actually focus better with some noise or movement around you?"** — don't explain Dunn's model or the project's theory first. You want their own unprompted self-description, because that's the exact mechanism (plain-language self-sort, no diagnosis) the whole project is built around testing.
- Don't mention ADHD/autism/diagnosis unless they bring it up themselves. If they do, follow their lead rather than steering back to a label.

## Handing it over

Say only: **"Swipe between the three screens, try anything, think out loud if you can."** Then stop talking. Let them find the swipe gesture themselves — if they can't find it in a few seconds, that's a real finding (write it down), not something to immediately rescue by pointing at it.

## What to watch for, per page (don't ask about these directly first — watch, then ask)

**Page 1 — Calm routing**
- Do they understand the calm-vs-fastest comparison without you explaining the colored bar?
- Do they pick the calm route or the fastest one? Ask why, after they choose, not before.
- Does the extra-minutes tradeoff ("+5 min") feel worth it to them, or annoying?

**Page 2 — Auditory regulation**
- Do they try Relax or Concentrate first? Does the distinction make sense to them, or does it feel arbitrary?
- Do they turn a sound on at all, or leave it off? If off, ask why — silence might be a deliberate reduced-stimulation choice, not disinterest.

**Page 3 — Haptic fidgeting**
- On iPhone: do they notice the vibration is missing before or after reading the note? Does the visual-only ripple feel like "enough," or like a broken feature?
- Which of the three fidget tools (tap/hold/pattern) do they gravitate to, unprompted?

## Questions to ask after they've explored freely (in this order)

1. "What was confusing, even for a second?"
2. "What would you actually use again, if this were a real app?"
3. "Is there anything here that felt like it assumed something about you that wasn't true?" — this is the single most important question for this project specifically, since it directly tests whether the plain-language/no-diagnosis approach actually avoids the mis-sorting problem the whole Define phase was built around.
4. "Anything that felt like it was designed for someone else, not you?"

## What not to do

- Don't correct them if they "use it wrong" — there's no wrong way; if the intended use isn't obvious, that's the finding.
- Don't defend a design choice in the moment. Write down the pushback and move on.
- Don't lead with the research (Dunn's model, the moderate-arousal study, etc.) unless they ask "why does this work like this" — if they ask, that's a good sign the mechanic itself raised a real question, worth noting.

## Immediately after each session (2 minutes, before you forget)

Write down: what they swiped to first, what they said unprompted, the one thing that confused them, and whether their self-described preference (quiet vs. noise) matched what they actually chose in the app. That last one is the real test of the project's central bet — if it doesn't match, that's not a failure, that's exactly the kind of finding Stage 4 was supposed to produce.
